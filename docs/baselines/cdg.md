> This note records a reading of the original paper and the official implementation; for the adaptation and settings currently used in this repo, see the [experimental protocol](../protocol.md).

- Official code: https://github.com/Accenture/CDG (the address given in the paper abstract; the repo exists and has an implementation; no LICENSE file (GitHub API `license: null`); Python; created 2026-05-18, last push 2026-05-24; 0 stars). - Category: single-trace logit (temporal dynamics of in-trace token confidence, head vs tail difference) + multi-sample aggregation (answer-level weighted voting)
- Access requirements: gray-box; needs top-k logprobs at every generated position (k=20, vLLM `logprobs=20`), no full vocab or hidden states; training-free; β needs a small number of labeled validation questions to estimate; no extra model.

## 1. Method design

**Core observation** (Sec. 3.2, Fig. 2): each trace is cut by normalized position into N=10 bins and the mean token confidence of each bin is taken; the confidence of correct traces rises from start to end (positive gain), while that of incorrect traces rises less or falls. The authors give a theoretical explanation based on GRPO training dynamics (Sec. 4, Thm 4.2/4.5/4.6, assumptions A1-A3, M1-M3; not a rigorous proof, it relies on a "simplified training model" assumption).

**Formulas** (the main-text equations are images; restated below from Sec. 3.1-3.3, Algorithm 1 and the text of Appendix C.4):
- Token confidence (Eq. 1-2) follows DeepConf's top-k approximation: C_t = −(1/K) Σ_{j∈K_t} log p(y_t=j | x, y_<t), K=20. (The "KL to the uniform distribution" in Eq. 1 is only motivation; what is actually used is the DeepConf Eq. 2 form.)
- Trace mean confidence C̄_ℓ = (1/T) Σ_t C_{ℓ,t} (Eq. 3).
- **Confidence Dynamic Gain** (Eq. 6): ΔC_ℓ = C̄_tail(P%) − C̄_head(P%), i.e. the mean confidence of the last P% of positions minus that of the first P%. Default P=10% (equivalent to the last bin minus the first bin when N=10).
- **Trace score** (Eq. 7): s_ℓ = C̄_ℓ + β · ΔC_ℓ.
- **Answer-level scoring** (Eq. 8, Algorithm 1): R(a) = |T_a|^α · mean_{ℓ∈T_a} s_ℓ, predicted answer â = argmax_a R(a). α∈[0,1] is the "count damping". α=1, β=0 reduces to DeepConf-Mean weighted voting; α=1 with mean=1 reduces to majority voting.
- **Hyperparameters**: α=0.5, P=10%, N=10 bins; β varies by model: β=10 for DeepSeek-R1-8B and gpt-oss-20B, β=3 for Gemma-3-27B and QwQ-32B (Table 1 caption, Appendix C.2). β selection rule (Sec. 3.3): let r_b = μ_C / Δμ, where μ_C = E_ℓ[C̄_ℓ] and Δμ = |μ⁺_ΔC − μ⁻_ΔC| (the difference between the mean ΔC of correct and incorrect traces, which needs a small number of labeled validation questions); empirically β ∈ [0.5 r_b, 1.5 r_b] is robust; the estimated r_b values are DeepSeek 7.87, gpt-oss 8.70, Gemma 6.64, QwQ 4.39 (Appendix B.2.3). The authors also state they use "rotating cross-benchmark calibration" (β is estimated on one benchmark and applied to the other three).
- **Scored object and values**: s_ℓ is a scalar for a **single specific trace**, and so is ΔC_ℓ; R(a) is a **question-level answer vote**. The paper's final objective is best-of-N answer-selection accuracy, not correctness prediction; it is not a probability and has no calibration mapping. C_t is on the order of 10-22 (same as DeepConf), so β is tied to the scale of the confidence.
- **Differences from the official code** (from reading `scripts/eval/exp_voting_methods.py`): (1) `compute_gradient(confs, percentile)` takes the head/tail P% **windows by token count**, `n_cut = max(1, int(n·P/100))`, rather than first forming 10 bins and then taking the first and last bin; the two are close but not identical; (2) traces shorter than 10 tokens return ΔC=0 and are discarded in `cdg_vote`; (3) the confidence comes from DeepConf's `confs` (−mean over the logprobs dict returned by vLLM, with k=21 if the sampled token is not in the top-20); (4) traces with no answer (empty `extracted_answer`) do not participate.

## 2. Dataset settings
- Main experiments use 4 competition math sets, 30 questions each (Appendix E): AIME 2024 (MathArena/aime_2024_I + _II), AIME 2025 (MathArena/aime_2025), HMMT Feb 2025 (MathArena/hmmt_feb_2025), BRUMO 2025 (MathArena/brumo_2025). The paper writes BRUMO as "Bulgarian Mathematical Olympiad", whereas DeepConf writes Brown University Math Olympiad; both come from MathArena, so this is likely a typo, and this project should not carry over "Bulgarian".
- Additional: GPQA-Diamond (Appendix B.7, Table 8, DeepSeek-R1-8B only, number of questions **not reported**).
- No train/test split; β is estimated across benchmarks (see above).
- Answer format `\boxed{}`; grading uses Dynasor's `math_equal` (plus `quick_parse`, which strips `\text{}` and compares single letters case-insensitively); the GPQA prompt is not public (Appendix B.7 admits that DeepConf's GPQA numbers cannot be reproduced exactly).

## 3. Experimental protocol
- **512 traces are generated** per question (L=512, 512×30×4 = 61,440 traces/model in total); budgets L∈{8,16,32,64,128,256} are subsampled from the pool of 512 (shuffled, 5 random runs); the small budgets L=5, 10 are in Table 4 (added at reviewers' request).
- Decoding (Table 9, all with `logprobs=20`): DeepSeek-R1-8B T=0.6, top-p=0.95, no top-k, max 64,000; Gemma-3-27B T=0.6, top-p=0.95, top-k=40, max 8,192; QwQ-32B T=0.6, top-p=0.95, top-k=20, max 32,768; gpt-oss-20B T=1.0, top-p=1.0, top-k=40, max 130,000 ("disabled the high reason setting").
- Prompt (C.3): DeepSeek-R1-8B uses the official (Chinese) system prompt; the other models append "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}." after the question. No few-shot in any case.
- Metric: voting accuracy (%). There is **no** AUROC/ECE/Brier/PRR evaluation of the CDG score. Discriminative power is measured only by Mann-Whitney U + Cohen's d on the difference of ΔC between correct and incorrect traces (Table 5; all 16 model×dataset configurations have p<0.001, d between 0.39 and 1.45, e.g. DeepSeek AIME24: μ⁺=0.98, μ⁻=−1.53, d=1.43; gpt-oss has the smallest d, 0.39-0.56). This test pools all traces together (N_corr reaches the order of 13k), which introduces pseudo-replication from within-question correlation. CDG vs baselines uses the Wilcoxon signed-rank test (112 paired observations = 4 models×4 datasets×7 trace counts, p<1e-10).
- Seeds: variance <0.09% (5 random subsampling runs). Compute: 8×H100, about 8,600 GPU hours (including development iterations).

## 4. LLM backbone settings
- DeepSeek-R1-8B (the paper only writes DeepSeek/DeepSeek-R1-8B; the exact checkpoint is not reported), gpt-oss-20B, Gemma-3-27B-it, QwQ-32B. All are reasoning/chat models, served with vLLM (`logprobs=20`); precision is not reported, and context is bounded by max tokens (see above).
- **Qwen3 is explicitly excluded** (Sec. 5.1): "its post-RL training involves entropy control, mechanisms that directly disrupt the confidence trajectory patterns and thus violate the assumptions". This directly affects us (the target model is Qwen3 thinking).

## 5. Main results and conclusions (brief, key numbers)
- Table 1 average voting accuracy (mean over 4 datasets, L=512): DeepSeek-R1-8B: Pass@1 75.8 / Majority 84.2 / DC-Mean 84.2 / DC-Tail 88.3 / CDG **90.8**; Gemma-3-27B: 25.7 / 35.0 / 35.0 / 40.0 / **41.7**; gpt-oss-20B: 66.5 / 82.5 / 84.2 / 85.0 / **85.8**; QwQ-32B: 69.7 / 75.0 / 75.9 / 78.3 / **80.0**; overall average 59.4 / 69.2 / 69.8 / 72.9 / **74.6**. CDG is +5.4 over majority voting, +4.8 over DC-Mean, +1.7 over DC-Tail. (Each cell has only 30 questions, and 1 question = 3.3 points, so small differences are statistically fragile.)
- Ablations: keeping only tail confidence and dropping start confidence ("No Start") lowers the average 74.6 → 70.2 (DeepSeek 90.8→84.2); the degenerate versions with α=1 (no count damping) and β=0 are both clearly weaker; the curve over P∈{5,…,30} is flat (Fig. 7); after excluding the `\boxed{}` token, the selection agreement rate is 99% (0.0% accuracy difference). CDG still leads when evaluated on short and long trace pools separately; correct traces are usually shorter (t-test p<0.001), but Gemma on HMMT is the opposite.
- GPQA-Diamond (DeepSeek-R1-8B): Majority 68.2 / DC-Mean 68.7 / DC-Tail 70.7 / CDG 72.2.
- Limitations (Impact Statement): β needs a per-model validation set; mainly math only; needs top-K logprobs.

## 6. Fit to this project's unified benchmark
- **Applicability**: very well suited to long-CoT math tasks with Qwen3 thinking; it only needs vLLM top-k logprobs (k=20) and adds zero extra forward passes at inference. In our benchmark it should be split into two levels: (a) a **single-trace correctness score**: s_ℓ = C̄ + βΔC, plus ΔC alone without β (β→∞) and C̄ alone (β=0, i.e. DeepConf-Mean), with AUROC/AURAC/ECE computed directly; this evaluation is one the paper **did not do**, as it only reports voting accuracy and the mean difference of ΔC; (b) a **question-level answer score**: R(a) or its normalization (R(a)/ΣR) as the confidence in the majority answer, which needs K samples (the paper uses L=8…512, cost = K full generations; zero extra cost if the benchmark already has K traces).
- **Main risks**: (1) the paper **excludes Qwen3**, on the grounds that entropy control breaks the head→tail rising pattern; ΔC may well have weak or sign-flipped discriminative power on Qwen3, which this benchmark should record as a "preregistered risk" and report as is, without tuning to rescue it. (2) The magnitude of β is tied to the token-confidence scale (about 10-22) and the per-model r_b, with best values ranging from 3 to 10; recommended protocol: treat β as a single learnable scalar, determine it on an independent validation split with the r_b formula or a grid search, and report both endpoints β=0 and ΔC-only, to avoid selecting β on the test set. (3) The head/tail 10% is computed by token count; for Qwen3 thinking the head restates the question and the tail contains the final answer and `\boxed{}`, so the scoring span (thinking segment or whole response) must be stated explicitly (the paper uses the whole response, including the final-answer segment). (4) For truncated traces (no answer output), the tail is not a true "end", so they should be marked incorrect with a fixed handling. (5) Only the top-20 approximation is used, and DeepConf's implementation may use k=21.
- **Cost**: 0 extra generations per question (single-response score); the answer-level version needs K full generations; no extra model; score computation is one linear scan over the logprobs.
- **Recommended faithful implementation**: vLLM `logprobs=20`, C_t = −mean(top-20 logprobs); ΔC with P=10% head/tail token windows (consistent with the official code; note in the docs that the paper describes 10 bins); α=0.5; β chosen on validation by the r_b rule (and report β∈{0, r_b, ∞}); at the answer level use math_verify for equivalence clustering (equivalent to the paper's `math_equal`); report same-protocol comparisons against DeepConf-Mean / Tail / majority, and note that CDG's gains in the original paper were obtained on 30 questions × 4 datasets.
