# CAPE: Response-Level Confidence under Answer-Consistency Constraints

Definition and proposition numbering matches the paper (the body `latex/sections/formulation.tex` has Propositions 1–2, the appendix `latex/sections/appendix.tex` has Propositions 3–8, sharing one counter); proofs are in Appendix A of the paper. For the protocol see the [experimental protocol](protocol.md); for approaches that were tried but not adopted see [failures.md](failures.md).

The paper's tables and figures are written directly into `latex/` by `python -m visualization.build`; numbers in the text are written by hand. This note states conclusions only; for concrete values refer to the paper tables, `results/summary.md` and `results/<model>/*.csv`.

## Paper storyline

Answer equivalence and mutual exclusion define the per-question feasible set of correctness; classical coherence theory on this set gives a projection repair that aggregates by answer and shrinks by cluster size. The repair guarantees that the per-response Brier score does not get worse, but the residual probability left to "none correct" is not guaranteed to be accurate. Residual clearing and the error-weight analysis of the two scores identify this boundary, which leads to CAPE's direct joint estimation of the answers and the none-correct event.

The body is organized as: structure and repair, repair boundary, CAPE, experiments. The experiments first test projection, then compare CAPE with raw and repaired estimators, and then test none-correct prediction and the difference between scoring targets. Fig. 2 is in the structure section and shows two paths under the same answer structure, projection and direct estimation; projection is not a preprocessing step of CAPE. The general dominance theorem and the projection-distance lower bound come from classical theory; the task contribution lies in the answer-induced set, the count-weighted repair, and its event-prediction boundary.

## 1. Structure

Existing methods treat the K responses to one question as K independent binary-classification objects. But on verifiable single-answer tasks, the correctness label z ∈ {0,1}^K has two deterministic structures:

- **(A1) Equivalence consistency**: equivalent answers have the same correctness; a response with no answer is necessarily wrong. This holds when clustering and grading use the same transitive equivalence relation.
- **(A2) Unique reference answer**: at most one answer cluster is correct. This holds when the reference answer is unique.

So z can take only m+1 values: none correct 0, or the indicator vector 1_{C_c} of some cluster c.

**Definition 1 (coherent confidence)**: 𝒞 = conv(𝒵), i.e. the vectors with equal values within a cluster (π_{c(k)}), 0 for no-answer responses, π ≥ 0 and Σ_c π_c ≤ 1. Coherent confidence is a prediction over "each sampled answer + one extra outcome ∅ (none of the sampled answers is correct)", with π_∅ = 1 − Σ_c π_c.

For any information 𝒢 that determines the answer clusters, the Bayes prediction E[z ∣ 𝒢] lies in 𝒞, and its ∅ coordinate equals P(z = 0 ∣ 𝒢). The prediction therefore splits into two parts:

- **Shape**: given by the verifier; it can be imposed on any estimator for free (principle 1: project).
- **∅ probability**: coherence constrains only its computational relation and does not determine whether the prediction is accurate; it must be learned and evaluated explicitly (principle 2), and can be implemented jointly or modularly.

Methods that score each response separately (DeepConf, SelfCertainty, ReasonProbe, ORM, etc.) give equivalent responses different scores; normalized voting (SC, CISC) satisfies Σ_c π_c = 1 and leaves no mass for ∅. The former may fall outside 𝒞; the latter is already on the boundary of 𝒞 but always gives ∅ zero mass.

## 2. Theory

Let S be a strictly proper score with differentiable entropy (Brier or log loss), with Bregman representation S(p, z) − S(z, z) = D_Φ(z, p). The tools are classical (Bregman projection, total variance decomposition, conditional logit); what is new is the set 𝒞 induced by answer equivalence and the following two consequences.

| No. | Result | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Proposition 1 (coherence dominance) | q* = argmin_{q∈𝒞} D_Φ(q, p) satisfies S(q*, z) ≤ S(p, z) − D_Φ(q*, p) for every z ∈ 𝒵. Under Brier, π*_c = (p̄_c − γ/n_c)_+, where γ ≥ 0 is nonzero only when the sum constraint Σ_c π_c ≤ 1 is active (the cluster means sum to more than 1); the improvement is ≥ ‖p − q*‖², with equality for the one-step "within-cluster average" | On every question satisfying (A1)–(A2), projection does not make any estimator worse, and the improvement has a lower bound that can be computed before looking at the reference answer (label-free lower bound). The sum constraint shrinks by γ/n_c in one step, with minority answers shrunk the most, so coherence itself fuses scores with vote counts. The dominance itself is the Bregman form of de Finetti's coherence theorem |
| Proposition 2 (event-score sensitivity) | For a coherent prediction and the true conditional posterior, the response Brier regret is Σ_c n_c δ_c²/K and the ∅-event regret is (Σ_c δ_c)²; the latter is ≤ H x the former, with H=KΣ_c1/n_c≤K², and the constant is sharp for fixed cluster sizes. When the labels satisfy A1/A2, the same bound also applies to the realized loss of a single prediction | Repeated answers introduce an n_c/K weight into the response score; the event score aggregates errors across different answers. With K fixed, response coherence still controls event coherence, but the guarantee is much weaker under finite error. The bound does not constrain the empirical loss difference of two predictions. |
| Proposition 3 (robust lower bound, appendix) | For any p, its projection q* and any z ∈ {0,1}^K (coherent or not): S(p, z) − S(q*, z) ≥ ‖p − q*‖(‖p − q*‖ − 2 d(z, 𝒞)) | When the equivalence judgment is wrong, the lower bound loses only one term linear in the distance from the label to 𝒞 |
| Proposition 4 (voting cannot express none correct, appendix) | If Σ_c π_c = 1, then on questions with no correct answer the per-response Brier is ≥ (K Σ_c 1/n_c)^{−1}; for a unanimous and wrong question Brier = 1 and log loss is infinite | Recalibrating the vote share can recover ∅ mass, but all unanimous questions still get the same confidence |
| Proposition 5 (vote-share floor, appendix) | F_K = F_∞ + E[Var(ρ(μ) ∣ v_K)], F_∞ = E[ρ(μ)(1 − ρ(μ))]; under regularity conditions F_K − F_∞ = η/K + o(1/K), η = E[ρ′(μ)² μ(1 − μ)] | The vote share is a binomial measurement of μ; more samples only remove an O(1/K) grouping loss; as long as P(0 < ρ(μ) < 1) > 0, F_∞ is positive |
| Proposition 6 (posterior under correlated evidence, appendix, with Eq. posterior) | Under Gaussian evidence with within-cluster equicorrelation r, P(c* = ∅ ∣ λ) ∝ ω_∅, P(c* = c ∣ λ) ∝ ω_c exp(β κ(n_c)(λ̄_c − τ)), κ(n) = n/(1 + (n − 1)r) | The Bayes prediction is a conditional logit with ∅ as an outside option, and normalized voting is a special case. For r > 0, κ ≤ 1/r, so CAPE takes the within-cluster evidence mean rather than the sum; r = 0 (sum) is what semantic energy does |
| Proposition 7 (per-response prediction misses ∅, appendix; informal statement in body §2.2) | In the correlated-evidence model of Proposition 6 (ν₁ > ν₀, ω_∅ > 0, all ω_c > 0), for m ≥ 2 the residual mass q_∅ that projection leaves for ∅ is 0 with positive probability, while the Bayes π^B_∅ is always positive | Split questions: per-response predictions of mutually exclusive answers over-claim, the sum constraint clears the residual mass, and the ranking is lost. "None correct" must be predicted jointly from the evidence of all responses |
| Proposition 8 (per-response prediction cannot identify ∅, appendix) | In the same model, given the clustering, the law of (λ_k, z_k) does not depend on r, while on unanimous questions π^B_∅ varies with r, so there is no fixed function f(p_{1:K}) that equals π^B_∅ almost everywhere for two values of r | Unanimous questions: a question-level dependent-expert problem, which loses only calibration, not ranking |
| shift-gated pooling (not in the paper) | Log pooling π(o) ∝ π^T(o)^{1−w} π^H(o)^w: (i) the output is in 𝒞 for any w; (ii) the log loss of every outcome is ≤ the w-weighted average of the two components; (iii) w = 0 falls back to π^T, and under exchangeability P(w < 1) ≤ α and E[ℓ] ≤ E[ℓ_H] + √α E[(ℓ_T − ℓ_H)_+²]^{1/2}; (iv) π is a weighted barycenter of two KLs | Linear pooling also satisfies (i)–(iii); log pooling is chosen because it adds evidence in logit space (consistent with Proposition 6) and is externally Bayesian. Validation questions have K=4 and test questions K=8, so exchangeability is only approximate |

The appendix also gives: the proof that the Bayes prediction is in 𝒞; the total variance decomposition for "breaking the floor" (a predictor observing 𝒢 ⊇ σ(v_K) has Brier = F_K − 𝓘); the form θ₁ = 1 − b/β in u_∅ implied by shared difficulty; that with multiple correct answers only the truncation needs (A2); and that projection gives no guarantee on AUROC.

## 3. CAPE (`cape/model.py`)

**Answer model**

  u_c = log(n_c / K) + mean_{k∈C_c} g(x_k)
  u_∅ = θ₀ (π_∅ = 1 when no response has an answer)
  π = softmax(u_1, …, u_m, u_∅); the confidence of response k = π_{c(k)}, and 0 for a response with no answer.

- This is the Bayes form of Proposition 6: the outside-option weight ω_∅ is a constant, and evidence enters ∅ only through normalization. The only deviation is that within-cluster evidence is averaged (the r → 1 limit), because the measured within-cluster correlation is far from independent; this makes u_c invariant to K at a fixed vote share, so K=4 training can be used for K=8 testing.
- The vote-count prior log(n_c/K) is the exchangeable prior (exponent η fixed to 1).
- There is a single evidence signal: g(x) = ψ_f·z, where z is the hidden-state probe logit and ψ_f is one weight learned per answer format (math / multiple choice / short answer) (the probe's discriminative power differs across formats; a single ψ is dominated by math and over-trusts the probe on multiple-choice OOD sets). The probe is a logistic regression on the first 256 whitened principal components of the training pool, using hidden states at two positions (a middle layer at the final-answer token, and the 3/4-depth layer at the end-of-reasoning token), with strength selected by cross-validation; training-pool responses use cross-fitted (out-of-fold) logits so that the answer model does not over-trust the probe. No token statistics, and no follow-up reasoning (P(True), verbalized confidence) are needed.
- Training: maximize the log-likelihood of "the correct cluster, or ∅ when no sampled answer is correct" (a strictly proper score over the m+1 outcomes), with L2 strength selected by validation log loss; about one minute on CPU.
- **Properties**: the output is always in 𝒞; with g ≡ 0 and ∅ removed it reduces to SC. At inference, beyond the K responses, each response costs only one extra teacher-forced prefill (to get hidden states).

**Three components, three ablations**: the ∅ outside option (principle 2), the vote-count prior (exchangeable prior), and the hidden-state evidence. Only ablations removing these three are reported; the other designs (support gate, token statistics, all 22 trace statistics, learned ∅ features, per-response loss, learned η) each have an effect below 0.01 on the training pool, the development set and earlier rounds of test sets, and are removed under the convention "below 0.01 counts as noise" and not reported as ablations (see [failures.md](failures.md) §3b). The support gate (shift-gated pooling) only acts on answer formats outside the training pool and is kept as an optional implementation (`CAPE(evidence="gated")`).

**Design and freeze**: round 5 (2026-10-20). CAPE was simplified to the form above using training-pool cross-validation, training-pool leave-one-source-out, and the development set `dev` (disjoint from the test sets and used only for selection); removing the gate and token statistics also referred to the effect sizes in the round-4 test-set ablations (all below 0.01), and is disclosed as a new design round. New evidence (cross-trajectory forced answering, jointly trained hidden-state evidence, etc.) is not carried over and is recorded in the [negative-results record](failures.md) §3b.

Round 6 (2026-10-20, OOD calibration): the per-format evidence weight ψ_f was added on the development set SuperGPQA (the OOD proxy) of the three backbones, and the probe positions were changed to the answer-token middle layer + the 3/4-depth layer at the end-of-reasoning token; it was evaluated once on the test sets and then frozen; diagnostics and variants not adopted are in failures.md §3c.

**Two implementations**: the two principles have two implementations, and the paper reports both. CAPE uses a conditional logit with an outside option to learn the distribution in 𝒞 directly and predicts ∅ jointly, without performing projection; the modular implementation is a coherent combiner (a projected per-response combiner with the same support gate) plus a separately trained ∅ classifier, followed by renormalization. The two principles matter more than which implementation carries them: on the confirmation set both lead all published estimators; with a small training pool the modular implementation is better, and with the full pool the two are close.

**Controls** (`uqbench/studies/controls.py`): the per-response combiner uses 22 trace statistics plus 5 voting features; then projection, the probe and the support gate are added in turn (the "coherent combiner"), and finally a separately learned ∅ classifier (the "modular implementation"). They use more evidence than CAPE and are reported as other implementations of the same two principles.

## 4. Main conclusions

**Conclusions and boundaries of round 6 (ψ_f + new probe positions) that can go into the paper** (test sets, mean over 30 pairs; the paired aggregate bootstrap resamples the questions of each test set jointly across the three backbones each time; positive values favor CAPE; `results/robustness_paired.csv`; improvements below 0.01 count as noise):

| Conclusion | Evidence | Boundary (stated as is) |
|---|---|---|
| Each of the two principles brings an improvement of ≥ 0.01 | Projection raises the AUROC of published estimators by 0.02–0.1; ∅ outcome: removing ∅ gives AUROC −0.037, Brier +0.021 | — |
| All three components of CAPE are necessary | Removing ∅: AUROC +0.037 / Brier +0.021; removing hidden-state evidence: +0.040 / +0.022; removing the vote-count prior: +0.012 / +0.011 | The other designs (gate, token statistics, learned ∅ features, etc.) have an effect < 0.01 and are removed |
| CAPE clearly outperforms published estimators | Against Sem. Energy, SC, Combiner: AUROC +0.023 to +0.042, Brier +0.013 to +0.024; against SEP / ReasonProbe + projection: AUROC +0.011 / +0.012, Brier +0.008 / +0.009; on OOD, Brier is significantly lower than all competitors | OOD AUROC is on par with Combiner (0.7907 vs 0.7911) |
| Coherent implementations under the same evidence differ from each other by < 0.01 | Against the coherent combiner (22 trace statistics plus the same probe, projected): AUROC +0.000, Brier +0.004 | We do not claim that CAPE outperforms other coherent implementations; consistent with Proposition 6: under the same evidence the Bayes prediction is determined by the evidence, and the gain comes from the two principles themselves |

- **Principle 1 (projection)**: for every published estimator whose output is not coherent, projection does not raise Brier on any (estimator, backbone, test set) combination and raises AUROC on most; the projected DeepConf exceeds all vote-based estimators. When the labels satisfy (A1)–(A2) the certificate holds per question, and the robust certificate holds in all checks (macros `\CohImprovedBrier`, `\RobustChecks`; figure `fig:coherence`, tables `tab:projection`, `tab:projsteps`). "Calibrated probability x vote share" reaches a similar AUROC but raises Brier on some questions, which projection does not.
- **Principle 2 (predict none correct jointly)**: voting ties on all unanimous questions and cannot separate consistent errors from consistent correct answers; CAPE's ∅ mass can (figure `fig:analysis`, table `tab:consistent-errors`). Under the same evidence, the coherent combiner predicts per response and leaves the residual mass not claimed by projection to ∅; the separate ∅ classifier and CAPE predict this event jointly. The effect comes from Proposition 7: on unanimous questions the residual mass ranks "none correct" comparably to CAPE, and the calibration cost of Proposition 8 is small; on split questions per-response prediction over-claims and truncation clears the residual mass (on a considerable fraction of split questions the residual mass of the coherent combiner and of projected DeepConf is 0, and the overall ranking of the residual mass of projected DeepConf is below random), where joint prediction lowers the event Brier (tables `tab:none-correct-full`, figures `fig:analysis-null`, `fig:analysis-supervision`). Joint prediction puts all questions on the same scale: the AUROC after pooling across strata is higher than the combiner's best read-out (1 − top answer), while within unanimous questions and within split questions this read-out is close to CAPE. A control biased toward the baselines: recalibrating either read-out on each pair's own half of the questions (which needs the target labels, whereas joint prediction is fitted only once on the training pool), 1 − top answer recovers most of the Brier gain. What works is joint prediction, not the loss: the same model as CAPE fitted with the per-response loss (`objective="response"`), or with the ∅ logit set to a constant (`null_features=False`), gives ∅ predictions comparable to CAPE (Proposition 6: evidence enters ∅ only through normalization), and on the confirmation set a constant ∅ logit even outperforms learned ∅ features. An in-distribution separately trained ∅ classifier is comparable to CAPE's ∅ (`failures.md`, "attribution analysis").
- **Overall comparison**: over the 30 pairs CAPE has the best average AUROC, Brier and ECE, which holds under every aggregation and under leave-one-source / backbone out (tables `tab:summary`, `tab:summary-full`, `tab:metrics-1`, `tab:metrics-1`, `tab:signif-1`). The closest existing work, semantic energy, ranks better than voting and worse than CAPE. On sources not in the training pool (GPQA, GPQA-Ext., SuperGPQA), CAPE has the lowest Brier (0.187) and ECE (0.101), and its AUROC (0.791) leads all published estimators and is on par with Combiner.
- **Coherence floor**: as K grows to 16 the floor decreases but does not reach zero, and the error rate of unanimous answers does not reach zero either (figure `fig:kscale`); after cross-fitted recalibration, CAPE's Brier on most pairs is below the floor attainable by voting (`tab:information`).
- **Decision**: escalating questions by ∅ mass captures more "none correct" questions (figure `fig:analysis-none`).

## 6. Relation to prior work

- **Clustered Self-Assessment** (Cao et al., Findings ACL 2026): uses answer clusters as options and adds "None of the above", reading the model's choice probabilities, without training. This is the closest precedent; CAPE's ∅ and answer posterior are learned jointly under a proper score, and Propositions 4, 7 and 8 explain why ∅ is needed and why it must be learned. The answer-set judge ablation is close to this method.
- **SC, SE, CISC and weighted voting**: use only consistency or normalized voting, and are bound by Propositions 4 and 5.
- **Per-response scoring methods**: violate 𝒞, and the projection of Proposition 1 can directly improve them.
- **Semantic energy**: targets the same consistent errors, using per-response logit energy; Propositions 7 and 8 explain why per-response evidence is not enough; the sum is the r = 0 case in Proposition 6.
- **Forecast coherence and forecast reconciliation** (de Finetti; Predd et al., 2009; Panagiotelis et al., 2021; Paleka et al., 2025): the general dominance theorem and the projection-distance lower bound come from classical theory; this work instantiates them on the 𝒞 defined by the equivalence and mutual exclusion of LLM sampled answers, obtaining a count-weighted repair and a label-free certificate computable per question, and analyzes the boundary of its ∅ prediction. Forecast aggregation with dependent experts (Winkler 1981; Clemen & Winkler 1985) corresponds to Propositions 6 and 7.

## 7. Limitations

- (A1)–(A2) depend on grading and clustering using the same equivalence relation: for math, after using rules plus an LLM equivalence judgment that does not see the reference answer, projection does not raise Brier on any of the 510 combinations; with grader-independent clustering (math by normalized strings, TriviaQA by NLI), TriviaQA keeps 95% of the projection gain, math keeps only about half (gpt-oss writes answers in varied forms), and CAPE still leads (`uqbench.studies.ablation --studies equivalence`). In open-ended generation 𝒞 is only an approximation, and the cost is given by Proposition 3.
- One generation seed per backbone.
- ECE is weaker under domain shift; a small amount of in-domain data improves it markedly and lets the gate open for new formats.
- The gate detects format and representation shift, not difficulty shift within a format.
- The gain depends on how informative the single evidence signal is: it is clear on reasoning models, and when the evidence is weak CAPE is close to weighted voting.
